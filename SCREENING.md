# SCREENING — Roast Your Fridge (u-suzy-one-line-one-laugh-roast-your-fridge-f1063521)

Authorisation: "gas in semua misi yang mepet, lu bisa cek sendiri" (user, 21 Nov 2026).

## Method

Per the writing-jokes skill: first ideas discarded (fridge hums all night, empty
fridge, phone battery dies — all banned first thoughts in the brief). Seams found
in specific, recognisable appliance details; punch word last; one joke per line.
Per the write-funny skill: "what's wrong here?" answered per candidate, filters
stacked (irony + hyperbole / character / analogy). Per stop-slop: no throat
clearing, no adverbs, no "not X, it's Y" scaffolding in the lines themselves.

Scored 1–5 on the judge brief's five criteria, in order:
1. Roasts a fridge or a phone in a single line.
2. Real laugh on a SPECIFIC, recognisable detail, not a generic insult.
3. Twist, surprise or double meaning paying off in the LAST few words.
4. Reads cleanly aloud as one line, no setup needing a second sentence.
5. Feels fresh, not a worn-out fridge joke.

## Notes on the scoring

- **#1 (winner):** the door alarm is a detail every fridge owner recognises
  instantly, and "a librarian guarding the cheese" lands the twist in the final
  words as a fresh image, not a simile anyone has heard. Reads aloud in one
  breath. Nothing in it is a worn fridge trope.
- **#2 (runner-up):** strongest twist of the phone lines — the double meaning
  of "robot" (CAPTCHA vs. a phone that already knows everything about you)
  pays off in the last four words. Loses one freshness point: "prove you're not
  a robot" is a known joke shape, even if this framing is new.
- **#3:** the 3 a.m. ice-maker gunshot is the most visceral fridge detail here,
  but "settling a score" is a simile rather than a snap, so C3 is a 4.
- **#4:** "hospice with better lighting" is a strong final image, but the
  dying-vegetables-in-the-drawer premise is a familiar bit, so C5 is a 4.
- **#6:** the fourteen-sauces detail is specific and true, but the ending is a
  shrug, not a twist (C3 = 3).
- **#9:** autocorrect mishaps are a worn genre (C5 = 3) despite a good HR payoff.
- **#10 / #11:** storage-full and screen-time are real details, but both are
  well-trodden phone complaints (C5 = 3).
- **#12:** leftovers + electricity is generic; the rent line is a mild twist.

## Winner

**#1 — "My fridge's door alarm screams if I browse too long, like a librarian guarding the cheese."**
